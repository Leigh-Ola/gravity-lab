Model
Gravity uses three-dimensional Newtonian N-body dynamics in astronomical units, solar masses, and Julian years with G = 4π² AU³ M☉⁻¹ yr⁻². The engine uses a hybrid symplectic scheme: general encounters use kick–drift–kick leapfrog integration, while strongly bound hierarchical orbits use exact two-body Kepler drift between external N-body kicks. This keeps close moons and planetary orbits stable even during aggressive fast-forward.
Black-hole radii use the Schwarzschild radius. Close passes can trigger a simplified tidal-disruption test. This is not a general-relativity spacetime solver, so effects such as frame dragging, exact gravitational-wave emission, and true ray-traced lensing are visual approximations.
Performance & accuracy
The simulator dynamically chooses physics substeps based on time rate, major-body count, tracer count, and measured frame time. Major bodies fully interact. Individually added asteroids/comets are dynamic tracers that feel every major body. Generated belts/clouds use exact Kepler propagation around their chosen primary for speed and long-term stability; if another major body becomes a strong perturbation, affected members are promoted to dynamic tracers.
Physical collision radii remain independent of minimum on-screen marker size. Tracer–tracer impacts are intentionally omitted for performance, while tracer impacts with major bodies remain active. Generated population members are collision-checked against major bodies once per rendered frame. At wide scales, overlapping important markers are separated along their real projected orbital direction; this is display-only and never feeds back into the physics.

Full N-body: both feels gravity and contributes its own gravity to other bodies. Use for stars, planets, moons, black holes, and anything whose mass should change the system. More expensive with many objects.
Lightweight tracer: feels gravity from full N-body objects but its own gravity is ignored by the rest of the system. It can still move through the system and participate in enabled impacts. Best for tiny comets/asteroids or large swarms because it is much cheaper.
